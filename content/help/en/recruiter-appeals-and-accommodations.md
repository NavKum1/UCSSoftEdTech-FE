Candidates can ask you to look again at a decision. That's an appeal. They can also ask for an accommodation, a change that makes the assessment fair for them, such as extra time.

## Appeals

Candidates appeal from their own results page and say what went wrong: a technical problem, accessibility, an integrity review they think is unfair, a scoring error, or something else.

1. Open [ui: Appeals] under [ui: Fairness]. The [ui: Open] tab lists the appeals waiting for an answer, with the candidate's reasons and the latest decision.
2. Select [ui: Evidence pack] to look at the work again.
3. Select [ui: Resolve], then choose [ui: Uphold the original result] or [ui: Overturn: the appeal succeeds].
4. Write the [ui: Response to the candidate (emailed to them)]. It needs at least 20 characters.
5. Select [ui: Resolve appeal].

Someone other than the reviewer who recorded the latest decision must answer the appeal. If that was you, ask a colleague.

If you overturn the result, open the evidence pack again and record a new decision. The earlier decision stays in the pack's history.

The other tabs show appeals that were [ui: Overturned], [ui: Upheld] or [ui: Withdrawn] by the candidate.

## Accommodations

Candidates can ask for extra time, a written defense instead of a spoken one, screen-reader support, or something else.

1. Open [ui: Accommodations] under [ui: Fairness]. The [ui: Requested] tab lists the requests waiting for you, with what the candidate needs and whether it's for one coding task or all of their assessments.
2. Select [ui: Approve]. Or write a [ui: Note to the candidate (required to decline, at least 20 characters)] and select [ui: Decline].

The candidate is e-mailed your decision. Approved accommodations appear in the candidate's evidence packs, so reviewers read the timing and the defense fairly. An approved written defense lets the candidate defend their code in writing instead of out loud.
